# ADR 0005 — The hit pipeline is a layered model: one typed fan-out message + per-datum controllers

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-07-19)
- **Date:** 2026-07-19 (runtime proof 2026-07-18; design settled + canon-interviewed 2026-07-19)

## Context

The hit pipeline (damage / knockback / hitstun / reactions) ran through `HitResolver` — a hand-rolled harvest that `GetComponents`'d a projectile's dealer components into scratch buffers, applied its own channel guard, and enforced non-reentrancy as a *comment*. Every new exchange edited the resolver, and the deal/receive halves of each datum lived in two components (`KnockbackDealer`/`KnockbackReceiver`, `HitstunDealer`/`HitstunReceiver`) plus a per-datum `Arena.*.Core` processor. Einar decided to restructure cross-component gameplay communication around a **layered model** and merged per-datum **controllers**, and to prove it by re-passing the incumbent's PlayMode guarantees. This ADR records the *validated* design — it diverged from the plan during execution (all Einar-directed) and is written against the shipped code, not the plan's intent.

## Decision

**(a) Two idioms, one criterion.** A **fan-out context** (many arbitrary systems respond to one event) earns a pooled, typed `ExecuteEvents` message **only at all four clauses: ≥2 unrelated responders, on the dispatch target's own GameObject, on the same peer, served synchronously.** A single-receiver **operation** is a direct `GetComponent` + method call — a message with a known singular receiver is dispatch theater. **Cross-peer fan-out rides a replicated `SyncVar.OnChange` (ADR 0004), never a message; off-hierarchy fan-out stays a C# event or a `MatchMediator` wire.** "Hit" is the one fan-out context that qualifies: `HitMessage` (one pooled type, one channel-guard functor in `HitMessage.Functor`, no dispatch class), dispatched by `HitMessage.Dispatch` to every `IHitMessageHandler` **on the source**.

**(b) Layers are vocabulary, not a depth budget.** Layer 1 = the context at its site; layer 2 = handlers (may `GetComponent` and call public methods); layer 3 = the operations they call (`ApplyKnockback`, `ApplyHitstun`, `TakeDamage`). **Any layer may dispatch a further context** (the anticipated explosion-over-N-victims is a layer-2 dispatch). Analyzability comes from the *typed message* — grep every site and implementor — not from depth arithmetic.

**(c) Per-datum `<X>Controller`s — a binding, predictive rule.** A `<X>Controller` **(1) owns state and (2) exposes an API to modify it**; it is the state-owning half of the dispatch model (messages fan out; controllers hold state and expose the operations handlers call). `KnockbackController`/`HitstunController` each merge their old dealer+receiver pair and inline their old `Arena.*.Core` arithmetic; `Health` → `HealthController` (resolving `Pawn.cs`'s use of one word for the component *and* the `Pawn.Health` SyncVar value). The rule earns its keep by **excluding**: `Pawn<X>` simulation shells keep the `Pawn<X>`/`<X>Processor` pairing (per-tick sim state, not dispatch-model state-owners), and `NetworkUIController` is thereby **known-misnamed** (a bridge owning no modifiable state — rename pending, out of slice scope).

**(d) The atomic hit sequence lives inline in `HitMessage.Dispatch`:** durability spent first (presence of a live `HealthController` on the source is the switch), then every handler runs unconditionally in component order, channel-filtered by the functor — so authored order can never change a hit's outcome, and a mid-hit projectile death cannot retract a handler. **Extraction trigger:** a second hit source (explosion, cast) *extracts* this sequence to a shared home, never duplicates it.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keep the incumbent** (`HitResolver` harvest) — every new exchange edits the resolver; non-reentrancy is a comment, not machinery. The pay-down here is deleting it.
- **Messages everywhere** (victim-side single-receiver message types) — 4 message types + a generic pool were deleted from the plan's own first draft; a known singular receiver is a direct call.
- **Split dealer/receiver components per datum** — no single home per datum; the merge gives one.
- **A damage/DamageTaken fan-out context** (Einar's own suggestion, declined on evidence) — real fan-out, but `CurrentHealth.OnChange` is already a cross-peer, engine-native, zero-owned-mechanism dispatcher at the correct site with per-subscriber filtering (ADR 0004). The message idiom cannot beat it by wrapping it: it reaches **0-of-4** cosmetic responders (they sit on child GOs by concern — `PawnColor` on `Body Mesh`, `PawnDamageVfx` on `VFX` — while `ExecuteEvents` is GameObject-scoped) and collects a **zero offset** (the responders share a *signal*, not a *predicate*). **General lesson: `SyncVar.OnChange` IS an engine-native cross-peer dispatcher at the correct site — you cannot beat it by wrapping it.**
- **A blanket `XController` rename** — rejected as a suffix applied on sight; the rule in (c) is a *definition with exclusions*, a different thing.
- **A runtime re-entrancy depth-guard, now** — deferred, not adopted; see Consequences.

## Evidence

- **Pay-down receipt (measured):** `HitResolver`, `HitContext`, `IHitHandler`, `KnockbackDealer`, `HitstunDealer`, `KnockbackReceiver`, `HitstunReceiver`, `InstantiableObjects` deleted; `Arena.Knockback.Core` / `Arena.Hitstun.Core` / `Arena.Damage.Core` asmdefs + processors + their EditMode tests deleted. Net ≈ 940-line deletion; the model itself also shrank at review (the depth bound and R1 were deleted, not added).
- **The four-clause R2 is calibrated, not asserted:** a sweep of all 12 multi-responder contexts found the one-clause form disagreeing with the honest one on 4 of the 5 it admits. Decisive case: `PawnManager.OnPawnKilled` satisfies the one-clause form exactly, yet promoting it **silently breaks the client-side ragdoll** — `PawnBodySpawner` subscribes in `OnStartClient` while the event is raised in a `[Server]` method (peer-disjoint; a synchronous local dispatch can never serve both). The same-peer and same-GameObject clauses exist to catch exactly that.
- **Guarantees pinned by PlayMode:** the incumbent's suite (`HitstunKnockbackTests`, `BotShootsEnemyTests`, `PassiveStealOnHitTests`, `OwnerProjectileEffectTests`, …) passes unmodified against the new pipeline (EditMode 240/240, PlayMode 55/55). Two new pins assert the guarantees `HitMessage.Dispatch`'s contract states: `HitDispatchOrderTests` (permuted handler order → identical outcome) and `HitAtomicityTests` (durability-1 bullet: damage + stun + knockback all land and the bullet despawns — handlers ran on a dead projectile).

## Consequences

- **`HitMessage` carries only the two context GameObjects (`Source`/`Victim`), the `Instigator`, and hit geometry** — every *component* a handler needs it looks up itself from Source/Victim, so no pooled component ref can go stale (the `Motion`/`SelfHealth`/`OtherMotion` fields the plan first carried were removed). This dissolves the "≈5 of 11 fields null on a blast" concern: those fields no longer exist.
- **Ricochet + Deflect are consolidated into `RedirectionController`** — one component branching on whether the victim resolves to a `NetworkedMotion` (mover → adopt its heading; surface → reflect across the normal). No per-channel config struct (nothing varies yet).
- **Owner-contributed on-hit behaviour is a spawn-time self-clone, not a hit-time harvest.** A passive's `IOwnedObjectContribution` adds its handler onto the owner's spawned objects at spawn (`SpawnContributionReceiver`); by hit time it is an ordinary handler on the source, so **`HitMessage.Dispatch` never resolves the ability system.** Why it can't be dispatched-to-the-owner instead: the pawn root carries dealer-capable controllers, so an owner dispatch would fire those as double-dealers, and filtering re-couples dispatch to `PawnPassiveAbilities`. This is acknowledged passive-system debt ([[passives-convoluted-refactor-later]]).
- **The re-entrancy cycle-guard is deferred debt, not shipped.** The plan named cycles (`X→Y→X`) as the residual risk of "any layer may dispatch" and specified a runtime depth-counter + `ReentrancyGuardTests`. Neither was built, because **no handler dispatches a nested hit today** — building the guard now is machinery for a code path nothing exercises (rule-of-two). **Repayment trigger:** the first feature that dispatches a nested hit (the explosion / Session 1) ships the depth-guard and its test with it, in the same change that first makes cycles possible.
- **Corrects ADR 0008's kept-cores list.** ADR 0008 listed `HitstunProcessor`, `KnockbackHelper`, `DeflectMath`, `RicochetMath`, and `DamageProcessor` as *kept* determinism-critical cores ("re-run on multiple peers → silent desync"). H1 inlined all five because they are **not** re-run on reconcile: the knockback/hitstun/damage/redirect arithmetic resolves **server-side and ships only the result** (a TargetRpc'd impulse, a `StunnedUntilTick` SyncVar) — the client never re-runs it, unlike the genuinely-reconciled `MovementProcessor`. *Supersedes ADR 0008's kept-list for those five cores only; the component-primary default and the determinism-critical/branchy exception stand.*
- **Named pay-down target left unaddressed:** by this ADR's own criterion, `LocalUIEvents` is 14 channels of single-receiver dispatch (13 with one subscriber, 1 dead) — the largest target the criterion names, not touched here.

**North-star rationale.** *Elegance:* a hand-rolled harvest + two-component-per-datum split + three Core asmdefs collapse into one engine-maintained dispatch (`ExecuteEvents`) + one controller per datum — mechanisms removed, engine-native beats owned, and the doctrine shrank at review. *Essence:* the controllers' two modifier registries are the seam every future upgrade-interaction (damage buffs, resistances) folds into, without new hit plumbing — the game's few systems interacting through data contribution, not bespoke couriers.
