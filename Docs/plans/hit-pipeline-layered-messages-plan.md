# Migrate the hit slice to merged controllers + typed messages over ExecuteEvents

## Context

Einar decided to restructure cross-component gameplay communication around per-datum **controllers** and the **LAYERED MODEL** (his refinement at plan review): fan-out contexts are ExecuteEvents messages; single-receiver operations are direct calls. The decision followed three rounds of design debate (including a bias-separated design-critic run); remaining objections were converted into binding design rules and new tests. This plan executes **slice one**: the hit pipeline (knockback / damage / hitstun), at production quality, with the incumbent's guarantees re-proven by PlayMode tests. Settled with Einar: **merged controllers** (KnockbackController/HitstunController own both deal and receive halves; Health stays the hit-point controller, Damage the outgoing-damage component — different data, no merge), **distributed exchange orchestration** (dealers respond to one Hit message as layer-2 handlers; HitResolver is deleted entirely — no dispatch class), **IHitHandler migrates now**, **no victim-side message types** (delivery = layer-2 direct calls into layer-3 operations).

The slice itself is **unchanged and still ships**: it deletes `HitResolver`'s hand-rolled harvest (scratch buffers, its own channel guard, non-reentrancy written as a *comment* rather than enforced) in favour of engine-maintained machinery — a real pay-down, and "engine-native beats owned" (`DESIGN.md`). What changed is the **doctrine it ships as**, on three points:

1. **R2 gains three clauses (the important one) — PROPOSED, pending Einar.** As written it had ONE clause (≥2 unrelated responders) but was calibrated on a sample of ONE (Hit). Hit has four properties; R2 named only the first. **Evidence:** a sweep of all 12 multi-responder contexts in the codebase found the stated criterion disagrees with the honest one on **4 of the 5** contexts that satisfy it. The decisive case: **`PawnManager.OnPawnKilled` satisfies the stated criterion exactly** (2 unrelated responders, open set) — and promoting it would **silently break the client-side ragdoll**, because `PawnBodySpawner` subscribes in `OnStartClient` (`Pawn/PawnBodySpawner.cs:11-14`) while the event is raised inside a `[Server]` method (`Managers/PawnManager.cs:106-110`). The responders are peer-disjoint; a local synchronous dispatch can never serve both. This was a methodology bug (Parnas: walk a concrete change through the seams), not an implementation bug.
2. **The 3-layer depth bound and R1 are deleted — EINAR'S EXPLICIT DECISION (2026-07-16), binding.** The bound was vacuous as written ("per context" — any chain relabels each hop as a fresh layer 1), and any repair strict enough to bind would have forbidden this plan's own named future (the explosion-over-N-victims extraction trigger is a layer-2 dispatch). Replaced by: layers as vocabulary + a runtime re-entrancy guard for cycles. **Net: fewer principles.**
3. **The idiom's real reach is two instances — PROPOSED, pending Einar.** Under the four-clause rule the codebase contains exactly **Hit** (migrating here) and **`SpawnBuffReceiver`** (4 same-GO responders — `NetworkedMotion`/`SpawnScale`/`ServerCollision`/`Health`). Rule-of-two, barely met. **`SpawnBuffReceiver` is NOT migrated now** — its trigger is the damage-buff channel (`DESIGN.md`'s named next design task, whose leading candidate is a 5th on-spawn multiplier). Migrate it *then*, and let the module-touch count settle whether it paid (incumbent predicts 3 modules, message predicts 2). Rule-of-two arriving naturally, not manufactured.

**Recommended for rejection — PROPOSED at the 2026-07-16 review, pending Einar's decision** (recorded with their evidence so they are not re-proposed from scratch; both were Einar's own suggestions, and the recommendation is to decline them): a **damage/DamageTaken context** — the fan-out is real but already has a better dispatcher (`CurrentHealth.OnChange`, ADR 0004: cross-peer, engine-native, zero owned mechanism, per-subscriber filtering). It fails the repaired R2 on every added clause: reach is **0-of-4** (`PawnColor` is on the `Body Mesh` child, `PawnDamageVfx` on the `VFX` child — a root dispatch reaches neither, and reaching them re-adds the `GetComponentsInChildren` harvest whose deletion is this slice's receipt); the offset is **zero** (the four responders share a *signal*, not a *predicate* — four different peer gates, decrease-checks in two opposite polarities, because each does a different job); at the *damage* context the responder count is **1** (`PawnDamageVfx` alone). Parnas recurring-decision count: **one** feature (cosmetics) → inline. Also rejected: an **`XController` naming convention** — `Processor` predicts an `Arena.*.Core` `static class` (13/13, enforced by asmdef), `Dealer`/`Receiver` predict an ADR-0002 exchange end (5/5); `Controller` predicts nothing (1/1, and it means "UI bridge" — `NetworkUIController`), collides with Unity's `CharacterController`/`AnimatorController`, and contradicts `CLAUDE.md`'s documented `Pawn<X>` shell + `<X>Processor` core pairing. `KnockbackController`/`HitstunController` keep their names — they denote a real merge (dealer+receiver, one home per datum), which is what earns the word.

## Binding design rules — THE LAYERED MODEL (Einar's, settled at plan review; **amended 2026-07-16**, see Amendments above)

**Two idioms, one criterion:**
- **Fan-out context** (many arbitrary systems respond to one event, open responder set) → a **message** over ExecuteEvents. "Hit" is the archetype: 5 unrelated responders. **Promotion criterion (R2) — four clauses, ALL required:** a context earns a message at **(1) ≥2 unrelated responders**, that are **(2) on the dispatch target's own GameObject**, **(3) on the same peer**, and **(4) served synchronously**. When an operation's responders multiply, promote it then — pull, never push.
  - **Cross-peer fan-out rides the replicated SyncVar `OnChange` (ADR 0004), NEVER a message.** ExecuteEvents is an in-process call; it cannot cross the wire. A message dispatched from a `[Server]`-only path reaches no client.
  - **Off-hierarchy fan-out stays a C# event or a `MatchMediator` wire.** `ExecuteEvents.Execute` calls `GetComponents` on exactly ONE GameObject; `ExecuteHierarchy` walks UP only (and is not used here). A C# event is *reference*-scoped and reaches a child, a parent, a canvas, or another scene; a message is *GameObject*-scoped. For a responder set deliberately scattered by concern, GameObject-scoping is the worse addressing model.
- **Operation** (one component's capability, many callers) → **direct coupling: GetComponent + public method call.** A message with a known singular receiver is a misuse of dispatch. Damage/knockback/hitstun *delivery to the victim* are operations, NOT messages.

**Layers are vocabulary, not a budget** (amended 2026-07-16 — the depth bound and R1 are **DELETED**):
- Layer 1: the context (`HitMessage`), dispatched at a site. Layer 2: handlers (`IHitMessageHandler` implementors) — MAY GetComponent (own GO or others, incl. `GetComponentInParent` on the victim) and call public methods. Layer 3: the public operations they call (`ApplyImpulse`, `ApplyHitstun`, `TakeDamage`).
- These name **roles within one dispatch**, not a global depth budget. **Any layer may dispatch a new context — this is not restricted.** The on-hit explosion this plan already anticipates (see the extraction trigger: "a second hit source (explosion over N victims, a cast)") IS a layer-2 dispatch, and `NestedDispatchPoolProbe` already tests nested dispatch. A rule forbidding what the plan tests for is a bad rule.
- **What the deleted bound was protecting is bought better elsewhere.** Analyzability comes from the **typed message** — grep every dispatch site, every implementor — not from depth arithmetic. A 5-deep chain of typed contexts is more analyzable than a 2-deep chain of untyped behaviour calls; the bound was scaffolding for a property the message type already guarantees.
- **The residual risk is CYCLES, not depth** (`X → Y → X`: stack overflow + pooled-message aliasing). Doctrine cannot catch that, so it is guarded at runtime instead of by a rule: **`ServerCollision.DispatchHit` keeps a re-entrancy depth counter that logs an error past a sane bound (8) under `UNITY_EDITOR || DEVELOPMENT_BUILD`**, compiled out of release. A guard, not a law.

**Carried rules:**
1. A message/operation input feeds a **recompute**, never an in-place edit of an effective value. Controllers keep (authored baseline, registries); removal = delete + recompute; `ResetState` clears registries, never baselines.
2. Consume-once data integrates at the **tick boundary** — `PawnMovement.PendingImpulses` stays a PUBLIC Family-A contribution surface (PawnMovement is now UNTOUCHED by this migration); `ApplyImpulse` ends in `movement.PendingImpulses.Add(...)`, a legal layer-3 data contribution.
3. `HitMessage` derives `BaseEventData`, `base(null)` (safe — verified in ugui 2.0.0 source), **pooled via static Get/Release on HitMessage itself** (only one pooled type — MessagePool<T> failed the inline rule), `Reset()` nulls every ref, Release immediately after dispatch; `HitMessage.Functor` statically cached with the channel guard baked in.
4. All gameplay mutation originates `[Server]`; the KnockbackController TargetRpc hop to the owning client (client-simulated movement) is the sanctioned delivery leg, identical to today.
5. Hit atomicity by construction: durability spent FIRST in `ServerCollision.DispatchHit`, then every handler runs unconditionally (even on a dead projectile); death gates only despawn/projectile-channel afterwards. Victim-side delivery is plain synchronous layer-2/3 calls — same proof status as today. ExecuteEvents' per-handler try/catch strengthens this.
6. Disabled component = silent (ExecuteEvents checks `isActiveAndEnabled`) — this IS the composability/strip hook; `ResetState` re-enables (`enabled = true`). NOTE: ExecuteHierarchy is no longer used anywhere (victim resolution = GetComponentInParent) — the reentrancy risk and its pins are DELETED from this plan.

## Merged controllers

**`PawnMovement` is UNTOUCHED**: `PendingImpulses` stays the public Family-A contribution surface; `Modifiers` untouched.

**Reaction handlers** (`RicochetHandler`, `DeflectProjectileHandler`, `InstantiateOnHitHandler`, `PassiveStealOnHit`): interface swap `IHitHandler`→`IHitMessageHandler`, `OnHit(in HitContext)`→`OnHit(HitMessage)`; serialized `enabledChannels` fields keep names — prefab data survives.

**`ServerCollision`**: drop `_damage/_knockback/_hitstun` caches; Awake mask fold retypes to `IHitMessageHandler`; both internal channels (world-hit path, projectile-overlap path) converge on pooled `HitMessage` → **`[Server] public void DispatchHit(HitMessage hit)`** → Release. `DispatchHit` IS the atomic hit sequence: (1) durability spend first (`hit.SelfHealth?.ServerLose(1)` — presence stays the immortality switch), (2) `ExecuteEvents.Execute(hit.Source, hit, HitMessage.Functor)`, (3) `Execute` at each owner passive instance GO (`PawnPassiveAbilities.GetPassives`, IsSpawned-filtered; `CollectHitHandlers` deleted). Public so the test rig's synthetic-hit probe calls it directly. Guard/dedupe, `IsDead()` post-hit gating, pooled despawn unchanged. **Carries the re-entrancy depth counter** (amended 2026-07-16): a static `int _dispatchDepth` incremented/decremented around the dispatch in a `try/finally`, logging an error past 8 under `UNITY_EDITOR || DEVELOPMENT_BUILD` — the runtime guard that replaces the deleted depth bound and makes "any layer may dispatch" safe. **Extraction trigger (recorded in the skill + ADR): a second hit source (explosion over N victims, a cast) EXTRACTS this sequence to a shared home — never duplicates it.**

## ADR 0005 (skeleton in Docs/design/0005-hit-pipeline-layered-messages.md)

_Draft content. The 2026-07-16 amendments are folded in below, but per the Amendments attribution note only the depth-bound/R1 deletion is Einar-approved; the four-clause R2 and the two rejections are proposed. **The decision IS the layered model**: (a) the two-idiom criterion — a fan-out context earns an ExecuteEvents message at **≥2 unrelated responders, same-GameObject, same-peer, synchronous (all four clauses)**; a single-receiver operation is a direct call; **cross-peer fan-out rides SyncVar `OnChange` (ADR 0004) and off-hierarchy fan-out stays a C# event / `MatchMediator` wire** — neither is ever a message; (b) **layers are vocabulary, not a depth budget — any layer may dispatch, and cycles are caught by a runtime re-entrancy guard rather than a rule** (the depth bound was vacuous as written, and any binding repair forbade this plan's own explosion extraction trigger); (c) merged per-datum controllers; (d) the hit sequence inline in `ServerCollision.DispatchHit` with the named extraction trigger (second hit source extracts, never duplicates).

Alternatives rejected: incumbent kept (hand-rolled handler harvest; every new exchange edits the resolver); messages-everywhere (victim-side single-receiver messages are dispatch theater — 4 message types + a generic pool deleted from the plan's own first draft); split components (no single home per datum); **a damage/DamageTaken context** (2026-07-16 — real fan-out, but `CurrentHealth.OnChange` is already a cross-peer, engine-native, zero-cost dispatcher at the correct site with per-subscriber filtering; the message idiom cannot beat it by wrapping it, reaches 0-of-4 responders, and collects a zero offset — the responders share a signal, not a predicate); **a blanket `XController` naming convention** (2026-07-16 — `Controller` predicts nothing and contradicts the `Pawn<X>`/`<X>Processor` pairing).

Accepted trades: dormant halves on pooled bullets (conditional SyncVar reset, no ticks); disabled=silent as the strip hook; ugui as gameplay dependency; contact damage still on IDamageable (future slice); **the idiom's honest reach is two instances (Hit, SpawnBuff-when-triggered) — rule-of-two, barely met, recorded rather than hidden.**

**Pay-down receipt: HitResolver + HitContext + IHitHandler + four components deleted; a hand-rolled harvest replaced by engine-maintained dispatch. The model itself also shrank at review — the depth bound and R1 deleted, not added to.**

**Consequences worth recording:** the pawn's cosmetic responders are **scattered across child GOs by concern** (`PawnColor` on `Body Mesh`, `PawnDamageVfx` on `VFX`) — any future "just dispatch it on the pawn" instinct hits this wall, because `ExecuteEvents` is GameObject-scoped while a C# event is reference-scoped. **By this ADR's own criterion, `LocalUIEvents` is 14 channels of single-receiver dispatch (13 with exactly one subscriber, 1 dead) — the largest pay-down target the criterion names, and it is NOT addressed here.**
